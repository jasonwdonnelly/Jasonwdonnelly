The setup

The purpose of this hypothetical case is to gauge analytical and strategic thinking. There is no single right answer, but many ways to approach a solution. It is deliberately broad. The goal is a good discussion of the thinking, not one concrete result.

Case description

PHARMA, a pharmaceutical company, has an established product on the market called RHEUMDRUG. RHEUMDRUG is a prescription drug, indicated for rheumatoid arthritis and for psoriatic arthritis. In 2026 PHARMA is launching its third indication: osteoarthritis.

A clinical study will be published to support RHEUMDRUG's efficacy in osteoarthritis. PHARMA wants to prepare for the launch and needs an impactful way of communicating the new data to rheumatologists once it is released. You are the Globant Strategist tasked with defining a multichannel content strategy and working with Copy and Design teams to craft the creative assets.

The three questions
  1. What type of information would be relevant to obtain on customers and competitors specifically?
  2. How would you define the content strategy to communicate the new data once released?
  3. How would you update the existing RHEUMDRUG website to reflect the new indication?

Plain-language note: "indication" means an official approved use. Rheumatoid and psoriatic arthritis are autoimmune types where the body attacks the joints. Osteoarthritis is the wear-and-tear type. "Rheumatologists" are the arthritis specialists this is aimed at.

How I read the brief

This isn't just adding a new use.
It's new proof landing in an empty room.

RHEUMDRUG is already part of the arthritis specialist's world. It is approved for two other kinds of arthritis, so the trust, the familiarity, and the sales relationships are already there. That is what we start with. The job is not to introduce a drug. It is to make one study land.

Osteoarthritis is the arthritis doctors mostly stopped expecting to fix. The usual care is painkillers, steroid injections, exercise and weight loss. Things that ease the symptoms but do not change the disease itself. Right now there are no approved drugs that actually slow it down.

So when a study shows RHEUMDRUG working in osteoarthritis, the real question is not "how do we announce it?" It is "how big is the claim allowed to be, and how do we make specialists feel the size of it without ever overstating what the data proves?" That tension runs through the whole case. Lead with the science, and let the ambition sit underneath it.

Question 1 · Customers and competitors
The question, as asked

"What type of information do you think would be relevant to obtain on customers and competitors specifically?"

What I'd want to know before writing a single word.

Two buckets. I am not gathering information to fill a slide. I am after the few things that would actually change the plan if the answer surprised me.

The customer: arthritis specialists (and who is around them)

01

What they believe

Do they even see osteoarthritis as theirs to treat, or as someone else's problem they have quietly handed off? The gap between what they believe now and what the new data says is the size of the job.

02

Where they get convinced

Medical conferences, the specialists they respect, journals, official guidelines, or the sales rep? That map decides which channels get the weight, not our preferences.

03

How high the bar is

What would a doctor like this need to see in the results to actually change a habit? Skeptical specialists here are a good thing, not a problem.

04

Who moves first

Doctors who already prescribe RHEUMDRUG are the easiest win. Then: comfortable-with-digital versus rep-loyal, and fast movers versus those who wait for the guidelines to change.

05

The real-world friction

How osteoarthritis patients even reach these specialists, and what gets in the way between a doctor wanting to prescribe and actually doing it, like cost and access.

06

Who else matters

Family doctors, bone-and-joint surgeons, insurers, patient groups. Specialists are the target, but they do not decide in a vacuum.

The competition: what osteoarthritis gets compared to today

The honest competition is not only other brands. It is the current habit: cheap generic painkillers, steroid injections, and doctors sticking with what they know. Everything we say gets measured against "what I already do that works fine."

THE HABIT

Painkillers, injections, exercise and weight loss

Cheap, familiar, backed by the guidelines, and focused on symptoms. This is our real rival. The answer is not "that is bad." It is "that does not change where the disease is heading."

SAME CATEGORY

Other advanced arthritis drugs

Drugs in the same tier as RHEUMDRUG own the specialists' attention today. Worth tracking how they talk about their results and safety, and whether any of them are eyeing osteoarthritis too.

IN DEVELOPMENT

The drugs still being tested

Dozens of companies (55 or more, per industry trackers) are racing to build the first drug that truly changes osteoarthritis. None have crossed the line yet. If we move first with real proof, we can define the category before it gets crowded.

THE NOISE

Where the conversation already happens

Conferences, journals, search, and increasingly what AI assistants say when a doctor asks about osteoarthritis options. Worth knowing how loud each rival already is.

Question 2 · The content strategy
The question, as asked

"How would you go about defining the content strategy to communicate the new data once released?"

One idea, built to travel: "Follow the Data."

The study is the campaign. Not a slogan wrapped around the study, but the study itself made easy to explore and hard to skim past. It is also the safest route. When the proof is genuinely new, the most honest message and the most convincing one turn out to be the same message.

The promise to the audience: we will never ask you to believe more than the data supports, but we will make sure you feel exactly how much it does support.

Built in three gears, set by one rule: you can't promote it until it's approved

In this world you cannot market a new use before it is officially approved. So the plan moves in three stages. What is useful and allowed shifts at each gate.

Stage 0Before approval

Set the stage

Before the drug is approved for osteoarthritis, we cannot promote it. But we can raise awareness of the problem itself, and how big the unmet need is. Line up the leading specialists, the medical team, and the conference slots. Build all the content and the data visuals now, so launch day is a release, not a scramble.

Stage 1The data goes live

Make the proof land

One coordinated drop: a conference presentation, the published study, an interactive way for doctors to explore the results, sales follow-up, and the updated website, all live the same day. One story, every channel, the same hour.

Stage 2Keep it going

Turn interest into prescriptions

Different content for different doctors. Deeper detail for the skeptics, practical "which patients is this right for" guidance for those ready to try it, and proof from respected peers for the ones on the fence. What each doctor engages with tells the sales team what to talk about next.

The engine underneath, where Globant's AI earns its place

This is not a buzzword. It is how a small, specialist audience gets a message that feels relevant to each doctor, without building forty separate versions by hand. Content goes in as reusable blocks, the system coordinates it, and it sends the right next thing based on what is working.

Reusable blocks

One approved content library, broken into building blocks: claims, charts, references. Each block gets its legal and medical sign-off once.

→
AI coordination

Assembles the right blocks for each doctor and channel, drafts versions for a human to review, and reads what is landing to suggest the best next piece.

→
Delivery

Website, email, conferences and sales all fire from the same signals. A doctor who dug into the long-term results gets a matching talking point from their rep, not a random blast.

The rule that keeps this safe: the AI speeds up how the message is built and personalized. It never writes a medical claim on its own. Every building block is approved by a human, and by legal and medical, before it can be used. That is what keeps "impactful" and "compliant" on the same team.

How we'd know it worked

Measuring a data launch, not a banner ad.

Impression counts are the wrong scoreboard for a specialist audience of a few thousand. I would measure how deeply doctors engage and whether they actually move, mapped to the three stages.

Reach

The share of the target specialist list we actually reached, not raw impressions.

Depth

Did they explore the data (clicks, time spent, study downloads), or just open an email?

Signs of interest

Requests for more information, meetings booked with reps, resources pulled, repeat visits.

Prescribing

New osteoarthritis prescriptions over time, tracked by doctor type. The number that actually counts.

For deeper discussion

Competitive war-gaming.

If the conversation turns to "what happens when the market reacts," here is how I would think about each competitor's likely move and our answer. Open each one.

+ The cheap painkillers doctors already use
Their move

No campaign needed. They win by default through low price, habit, and "what I do already works well enough." Insurers may back them by pushing cheap generics first.

Our answer

Do not attack symptom relief. Reframe the whole category. Make it a question of "just managing the symptoms" versus "actually treating the disease," tied strictly to what the data shows. Cost-effectiveness and long-term-benefit content for the insurers.

+ Other advanced arthritis drugs
Their move

A rival in the same drug tier could rush its own osteoarthritis study, lean on a safety comparison, or simply outspend us at conferences and drown out our message.

Our answer

Own "first with real osteoarthritis proof" while it is true. Being first and defining the category is the advantage. Pair the results story with an honest, upfront safety and "right patient" message so a rival can't win the safety comparison by default.

+ The experimental drugs still in development
Their move

Dozens of companies are racing to build the first true disease-changer for osteoarthritis. If one posts strong late-stage results, our "we defined the category" story suddenly has competition, and headlines.

Our answer

Use the head start. Plant RHEUMDRUG in the osteoarthritis conversation now. Build relationships with leading specialists and guideline bodies, and an evidence-led brand story, before the field fills up. Be the known name while the newcomers are still "promising."

+ The rules, and the legal and medical sign-off
The obstacle

Not a competitor, but the toughest gate. Nothing can be promoted before approval, every claim needs proof and balance, and AI-built content invites extra scrutiny.

Our answer

Build the legal and medical sign-off into the system from day one. Approve once, reuse many times. Keep "before approval" awareness content and "after approval" product content strictly separate. The AI only assembles from pre-approved blocks. Handled this way, the rules become a speed advantage instead of a brake.

The close

PHARMA doesn't need us to make osteoarthritis sound exciting. It needs us to make one study impossible to miss, easy to trust, and everywhere the specialist looks, all in the same week and the same voice. That is a content problem, and it is the kind I'd love to solve here.
