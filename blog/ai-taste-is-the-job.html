AI Writes Confident Garbage. Taste Is the Only Thing That Saves You.

Everyone keeps asking if AI is going to replace writers.

Wrong question. And the fact that it's the question everyone asks tells you most people don't understand what a writer's job actually was.

The job was never typing. The job was judgment. And judgment is the one thing the machine can't hand you, no matter how good it gets.

The garbage is confident, and that's the trap

Here's the thing that makes AI dangerous, and it's not that it's bad.

It's that it's confident.

The machine will write you a paragraph that sounds like a real writer wrote it. Rhythm, structure, a little flourish at the end. It reads like competence. And underneath, it means nothing, or it means the wrong thing, or it's a smooth lie, and the machine has no idea, because it has never once felt the difference.

That's the trap. Bad writing that looks bad is easy to reject. Bad writing that looks good is how you end up shipping something empty and never noticing. The confidence is the poison. It gets past your guard by wearing the costume of the thing you're trying to make.

Taste is the accumulated damage

So what saves you? One thing. Taste.

And I want to be precise about what that word means, because the industry uses it like a vibe. It's not a vibe.

Taste is the accumulated damage of writing badly for years and learning to feel sick about it. It's every awful sentence you ever wrote and later winced at. Every joke that died. Every line a good editor cut that you fought for and were wrong about. All of that pain compresses down into a reflex, and the reflex is this: you read a sentence, and something in you flinches, because you've felt that specific wrongness before and you never want to feel it again.

The machine has zero of that. It has never been embarrassed. It has never lain awake over a bad ending. It has no scar tissue, so it has no flinch, so it cannot tell gold from garbage. It can only tell likely from unlikely, which is a completely different thing.

The job now

So here's the job, and it didn't disappear, it got harder to fake.

The machine drafts. You flinch. You cut. You rebuild.

I use AI constantly. A lot of my output starts as machine draft. And I'm not asking it for judgment when I do that. I'm the judgment. It's the fastest, most tireless intern in the world, and interns don't get to decide what ships. I do. It writes the confident garbage and I sit there and feel sick at the right parts and carve until something honest is left.

That process is the entire value now. Not the words. The choosing. The writers who get replaced aren't the ones who used AI. They're the ones who were already writing like a tool. Safe. Smooth. Forgettable. The machine does that version better, faster, no lunch break. If your writing had no flinch in it to begin with, yeah, the machine is coming for exactly that.

But the weird line, the true line, the one that makes a stranger stop scrolling and go "wait, who wrote this." That's still yours. That was always the whole job. It just got harder to hide behind volume.

The novel I wrote about the failure mode

I got obsessed with the version of this that goes wrong, so I wrote a novel about it.

It's called No Action Required, and it's a quiet horror story set inside a company obsessed with efficiency. There's a tool that only recommends. It never decides. It just makes the smart suggestion, every time, and the suggestions are good, so people take them.

And slowly, without anyone choosing it, the humans stop deciding. A dashboard replaces doubt. A document nobody reads transfers the responsibility. Friction disappears, and so does something harder to name. The book is about the moment a company outsources its judgment one convenience at a time and doesn't notice until the judgment is gone.

I wrote it, fittingly, with a lot of AI help, under my Sham Acumen name. That's not a contradiction. That's the exact tension the book is about. The tool is useful and it costs something, at the same time, and if you can't hold both of those in your head you should not be touching it.

The takeaway

Don't ask if AI will replace you. Ask if you built the flinch.

If you did, the machine is the best thing that ever happened to your output. It handles the typing. You handle the only part that was ever worth paying for.

If you didn't, no tool is going to save you, because the thing that's missing isn't speed. It's you, being home, deciding what's true.

Go build the flinch. It's the only job that's left, and it was always the real one. 😘
