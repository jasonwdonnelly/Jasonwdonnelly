Personality Tests Are a Comfortable Lie

You are not an INFJ.

You are not a Type 3, an Enneagram 8 wing 7, a Manifesting Generator, or whatever the quiz told you at the bottom of the funnel right before it asked for your email.

You're a person. Personhood doesn't fit in a four-letter box, and the fact that it feels so good when it seems to should make you suspicious, not comforted.

Here's the uncomfortable version.

We don't take personality tests to understand ourselves. We take them to be excused from ourselves.

Why the label feels so good

A label is a permission slip.

"I'm just an introvert" gets you out of the party. "I'm a Type 4, we're the sensitive ones" gets you out of the feedback. "That's not my personality" gets you out of the hard thing you didn't want to do anyway.

The test didn't reveal you. It gave you a costume with a note pinned to it that says, officially, you can stop trying now.

And who wouldn't want that? Growth is exhausting. It's so much easier to be a fixed type than a work in progress. A type is finished. A type has a chart.

The part that's actually a cage

Here's where the comfortable lie turns into a real problem.

Once you believe your personality is a fixed thing you are, every hard change becomes a fight against your own nature. Of course you can't do the scary thing. It's not you. Of course you avoid conflict. That's your type.

You've taken a snapshot of your worst habits on an average Tuesday and framed it as your soul.

But you are not fixed. You never were. You're a running argument between who you've been and who you're deciding to be, and the "type" is just the last few years of choices, wearing a lanyard, pretending to be destiny.

The label doesn't describe the cage. The label is the cage. You built it out of a quiz and you decorated it with words like "authentic."

What's actually true

You have patterns. That part's real. Tendencies, defaults, the grooves your reactions slide into when you're tired.

But a pattern is a habit, and a habit is a thing you can watch, question, and slowly rewire. That's the good news the personality industry can't sell you, because there's no assessment for it and no certification to hang on the wall.

The introvert can learn to work a room. The conflict-avoider can learn to say the hard thing. The "I'm just not a numbers person" can learn the numbers. Not by fighting their "type," but by noticing the story they keep telling and choosing, once, to tell a different one. Then again. Then again.

That's less flattering than a four-letter identity. It's also the only version that leaves the door open.

The book I wrote about this

I got so annoyed by all of this that I wrote a whole satire about it.

It's called Gripped: Your Personality Is What's Holding You Back. It's a novel, not a lecture, because I've never once been talked out of a comfortable lie by someone lecturing me. Stories get in under the door. Arguments just bounce off it.

Gripped takes the self-improvement industry, the personality-as-destiny racket, the whole business of paying strangers to tell you who you are, and it turns it up until the absurdity shows. It's the book that made me realize the thing "holding you back" is almost never your personality. It's your attachment to your personality.

There's a difference. That difference is the whole book.

So here's your permission slip

Since we clearly love those, let me give you one that actually helps.

You are hereby permitted to stop being your type.

You can keep the parts that serve you and quietly retire the ones that don't. You don't need a new assessment. You don't need to "discover your true self." Your true self is not buried treasure. It's a decision you get to make again tomorrow.

The quiz was never going to tell you who you are.

Only what you do next can.

Go do something your "type" wouldn't. 😜
