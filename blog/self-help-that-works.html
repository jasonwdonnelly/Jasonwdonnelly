Most Self-Help Is a Scam. Here's the Version That Works.

The self-help industry has a problem it can never fix.

If it actually helped you, you'd stop buying it.

So it can't. Not really. The whole machine runs on keeping you one book away from the version of you that finally has it together. Close enough to hope. Never close enough to leave.

That's not a conspiracy. It's just a business model. And once you see it, you can't unsee it.

The tell

Here's how you spot the scam version.

It sells you a feeling, not a change. You read it, you get the warm rush of "yes, this, I'm going to be different now," you highlight three lines, and then Monday happens and nothing is actually different. But the rush was real, so you go looking for it again. In the next book. The next course. The next guru with a nice jaw and a morning routine.

You didn't get helped. You got dosed.

The other tell is the word "just." Just wake up earlier. Just be present. Just choose happiness. As if the only thing standing between you and a transformed life was a decision you were too lazy to make.

If it were that simple, you'd have done it already. You're not lazy. The advice is just useless, dressed up as profound.

Why "just be positive" makes it worse

The cruelest trick in the genre is the positivity one.

Feeling bad? You're manifesting wrong. Struggling? Check your mindset. Not rich yet? You don't want it badly enough.

Now you've got the original problem, plus a new one. The shame of failing at the thing that was supposedly one mindset away. The industry sold you the disease and the cure in the same book, and the cure was the disease.

Real life is not a vibration you can raise your way out of. Sometimes things are hard because they're hard. Naming that is not negativity. It's the first honest thing anyone's said to you all day.

The version that actually works

So what does work? Not much that fits on a mug. But here it is, free, no funnel.

Do the thing. That's most of it. Not the version where you optimize your morning to prepare to eventually do the thing. The thing. Badly, if you have to. Motion beats mindset every time, because action changes how you feel, and feeling almost never changes what you do.

Shrink it until it's stupid. Not "get healthy." Put your shoes by the door. Not "write a book." One bad paragraph. The self-help industry sells you the mountain. The work is the next single step, and the next, and it's boring, and that's why nobody can package it.

And stop consuming advice about the thing instead of doing the thing. Reading about writing is not writing. Watching productivity videos is the most productive-feeling way to be completely unproductive ever invented. At some point you have to close the tab and be bad at something on purpose.

That's the whole method. It's unsexy. It's why it works and why nobody's getting rich teaching it.

The books I wrote about it

I got sick enough of the genre that I wrote against it. On purpose. Loudly.

Shut the F&%# Up: 200 Ways to Cure Unhappiness with Expletives is exactly what it says. Two hundred sharp, profane little jolts to short-circuit the noise in your head, because sometimes you don't need a framework, you need someone to interrupt the spiral before it wins.

How the F&%# to Human is the field guide nobody handed you. How to be a person, for anyone going through it for the first time, which is everyone, always.

And under my Sham Acumen name, I made a whole rack of one-step guides. Instant Cool. The Easiest End-All Way to Finally Lose the Weight. Fuck That. They poke fun at the "one weird trick" promise by taking it to its stupid logical end. The joke is the point. The joke is also, occasionally, true.

None of them will fix you. That's the difference. They're not trying to keep you broken and buying. They're trying to make you laugh hard enough to put the book down and go live.

The best self-help ends with you closing the book.

So close it. Go do the thing. 😘
