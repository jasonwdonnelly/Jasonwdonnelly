The best line is a caption

"We. are. airport. people." is the most differentiated asset on the entire site, and it's treated as a video subtitle. The hero leads with "mitigate capacity congestion" instead, which is exactly the generic SaaS phrasing every competitor also uses. Lead with the human truth. Defer the jargon.

The CFO's answer is buried

The proof that closes the deal, the 10% personnel-cost cut and the extra flights per terminal, sits below the fold. On a homepage, the buyer's whole business case should be the first thing they see, in their own units, not a reward for scrolling past the features.

Finding 03 · The maze

Nine "Better" modules, one "where do I start?"

The sales motion is "start where the pain is biggest." The information architecture doesn't reflect that. Reorganize the entry points around problems (security queues, baggage chaos, capacity ceilings), not product names. Let buyers self-identify by their worst day.

Finding 04 · The library

A rich resource hub with no current

Blog, news, case studies, e-books, webinars: genuinely strong inventory, presented flat. No clear journey, nothing tied to buyer stage, and (per G03) not structured for AI discovery. A library is not the same as a path.

Finding 05 · The platform "Airport people" is a brand, not a tagline

The single highest-leverage move on the whole site is to take that one true, ownable idea and build the homepage, the about page, and the entire content voice on top of it. Right now it's a flourish. It should be the foundation. The platform, not the garnish.

Caveat worth saying out loud in the room: this is an outside read of the front end, not the analytics. The fixes are hypotheses to validate against their funnel data, not verdicts.

The ground crew: a Reddit play for the people who are actually there

First, the truth that kills most Reddit strategies before they start: Reddit despises marketers. A B2B SaaS brand posting promo there gets flamed, downvoted, and screenshotted as a warning to others. So this is not a posting plan. It's something better.

The people who actually run airports are on Reddit being honest in a way they never are on LinkedIn. Controllers, ground handlers, gate agents, TSA and security staff, ops planners. They trade war stories, complaints, and dark humor in communities built around aviation and the working life of a terminal. That is the unfiltered voice of the exact people "airport people" claims to be. Three layers, in order of safety:

Layer 1 · Listen (the only layer with zero risk)

Treat these communities as the richest qualitative research source in the category. Mine the language, the recurring pain, the inside jokes, the things operators say when no vendor is watching. Feed that straight into every other channel so the brand sounds like it has actually stood on a jet bridge at 5am. Reddit as an input, not a channel. This alone is worth the effort.

Layer 2 · Show up as humans, never as the logo

Not the brand account shilling. The ex-operator consultants from G05, participating as real people with disclosed identities, answering genuine questions in threads where they have actual expertise, and never once pitching. The reputation you're building is "those people clearly get it," earned one helpful comment at a time.

Layer 3 · Contribute something the community would actually upvote

Rare, high-bar, value-first. A genuinely useful data visualization. An AMA with a named operator about what running a terminal is really like. The kind of thing that gets awarded, not reported.

The guardrailOne whiff of astroturf and "airport people" is dead everywhere

Real humans. Real handles. Value first. No links unless asked. The credibility you'd risk here is the same credibility the entire brand is built on, so the rules are absolute. Done right, you're the only company in the category with genuine standing where the actual operators talk. And being first to earn that trust is almost impossible for a competitor to undo.
