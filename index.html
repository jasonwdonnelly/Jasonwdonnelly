Moxso.

In-house at Moxso, a Danish cybersecurity company, I built the content operating system: AI infrastructure that runs the company's entire LinkedIn presence in four distinct voices, on a weekly cadence, to editorial standards the machine doesn't get to vote on. What's below is the architecture. The contents belong to Moxso.

A published LinkedIn carousel for Moxso: seven slides that walk a reader from a comfortable assumption to an uncomfortable question, and land on the category. Built to be swiped, not skimmed.

← Swipe through all seven →

The brief that armed the sales floor: six pages connecting product, market, and competitive strategy into something a salesperson could use in a live call. You can see the shape of the thinking. The words stay internal. That's the deal.

Sixteen interlocking modules, every one loading from a single positioning core. Campaigns, sales, partners, design, localization: nothing ships unless it agrees with the spine. That's the difference between prompting and infrastructure.

Voice profiles for three executives and the brand, built from interviews, post libraries, and rules with teeth. Raw notes go in. Posts come out sounding like the person, not the model.

The system in production: ghostwriting for the C-level. Two executives, two voices that don't sound like each other and don't sound like a machine. The CTO got satire with teeth. The CEO got conviction without corporate filler. Their stories, my pen.

Ghostwriters don't usually get receipts. The CEO posted one.

A weekly engine that decides what gets posted, when, by whom, and why, then produces the full set to standard. The week runs whether I'm in the room or not. That was the point.
